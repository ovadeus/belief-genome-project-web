01 — The Scoring Scale

A 10-Point Scale That Isn't Linear

Every dimension in The Belief Genome Project is scored from 0 to 9. But the scale is not a simple left-to-right slider from "disagree" to "agree." The endpoints represent strong, committed orientations. The middle — the score of 5 — is the most theoretically significant point on the entire scale.

A score of 5 does not mean indifference, apathy, or a failure to engage. It represents genuine quantum superposition: the belief is simultaneously held in two orientations and has not yet been collapsed by a forcing event. This distinction is what separates The Belief Genome Project from standard Likert-scale surveys — and from every prior attempt to map belief at population scale.

The Belief Genome Project is built on the principles of Quantum Theory. The Superposition Principle holds that a score of 5 is the most informationally dense score on the scale. It signals a belief system under live tension — not neutrality. It predicts that a forcing event (an election, a crisis, a personal loss) will cause a sharp, directional collapse.

02 — Connection Types

Four Types of Dimensional Relationship

When a score is recorded on one dimension, it exerts measurable influence on connected dimensions. The strength and direction of that influence depends on the relationship type. The Belief Genome Project defines four connection types, each with a precisely quantified weight multiplier — derived not from convention, but from the theoretical properties of how beliefs structurally depend on one another.

These weights reflect a core insight of The Belief Genome Project: belief systems behave like physical fields. Influence falls off with relational distance, and opposing beliefs create active interference rather than simple cancellation — just as waves in quantum systems do.

Type Weight Meaning
Direct ×0.75 Strongest pull The two dimensions share a core theoretical assumption. A score on one operationalizes or grounds the other. Near-predictive relationship.
Related ×0.45 Moderate pull The dimensions share overlapping conceptual territory but have independent variance. Connected through a common concern, not a common foundation.
Indirect ×0.20 Weak background pull A contextual relationship. The dimensions interact through a third mediating concept. Influence is present but easily overridden by direct connections.
Opposing ×−0.60 Inverse pull A high score on dimension A actively predicts a lower score on dimension B. These represent genuine philosophical tensions in belief architecture.
02b — The Core Formula

How Influence Is Calculated

The influence a source dimension exerts on a target is a simple product. But the interesting behavior emerges when multiple source dimensions all influence the same target — they interfere with each other, constructively or destructively.

Single Connection Influence
influence = source_score × weight
Example: source = 8, type = direct
influence = 8 × 0.75 = 6.00
Multi-Source Weighted Score (Interference)
weighted_score = Σ(score_n × w_n) / Σ|w_n|
The denominator normalizes by the sum of absolute weight values,
keeping the result within the 0–9 scale.

Opposing weights subtract — they don't just reduce,
they actively pull in the opposite direction.
Superposition Score (score = 5)
influence = 5 × w = midpoint pressure
A superposition score exerts neutral influence — it neither pulls
the target high nor low. It preserves the target's own superposition
state until a more committed score arrives.
This is the quantum analog: unmeasured = unforced.

The most significant analytical insights in The Belief Genome Project emerge not from single connections but from convergence patterns — when three or more dimensions all point toward the same target. This is the belief-system analog of wave interference in quantum mechanics, and it is the mechanism that allows The Belief Genome Project to detect internal contradictions that standard surveys cannot see. Signals in the same direction amplify into high-coherence profiles; opposing signals cancel or create measurable instability.

05 — Why This Architecture

Why Not Just Ask More Questions?

Traditional belief surveys ask questions in isolation. They assume each answer is independent. The Belief Genome Project's weighting system rejects this assumption entirely — it is the architectural difference that makes the framework a genome rather than a questionnaire.

Human belief systems are relational architectures. A person's score on Skepticism Level is not informationally independent from their score on Evidence Standards. They share conceptual foundations. To treat them as independent is to throw away the most valuable signal in the system. The Belief Genome Project encodes these relationships directly into the scoring engine.

The weighting system is the mechanism by which The Belief Genome Project captures belief coherence — the degree to which a person's scores form a structurally consistent whole — versus belief tension — the degree to which scores create internal interference patterns that signal instability or susceptibility to change.

05b — Coherence vs. Tension

Reading the System's Output

Two profiles with the same average score can have radically different structural properties — and this is precisely what The Belief Genome Project is designed to surface:

High Coherence Profile: Scores on connected dimensions reinforce each other. Direct connections show high correlation. The belief system is stable, internally consistent, and resistant to change. Forcing events cause confirmatory responses, not belief collapse. The Belief Genome Project identifies these as structurally entrenched profiles.

High Tension Profile: Multiple opposing connections are activated simultaneously. The system contains unresolved interference. The person may hold politically libertarian values alongside strong authority/loyalty moral foundations — a classic destructive interference pattern that The Belief Genome Project can detect and quantify. A single forcing event can produce rapid, dramatic belief collapse.

The 5-score superposition zones are particularly diagnostic. A cluster of 5-scores in connected dimensions is not a sign of an uncommitted respondent — it is the signature The Belief Genome Project reads as a belief system in active tension, poised for collapse.
